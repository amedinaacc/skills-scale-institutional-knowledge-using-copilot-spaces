# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

## Project Management Processes Summary

OctoAcme follows a structured, lifecycle-based approach to project management grounded in clear ownership, customer-first principles, and iterative delivery. The process flows through five distinct phases: **Initiation** (validating business need and aligning stakeholders), **Planning** (breaking work into shippable increments and defining dependencies), **Execution** (building, testing, and iterating daily), **Release** (deploying to production with standardized controls), and **Close & Retrospective** (capturing learnings for continuous improvement). Each phase is anchored by key artifacts—from the Project One-pager and risk register to acceptance criteria and release notes—that serve as a single source of truth across cross-functional teams.

The organization emphasizes **clear role definition and accountability**. Three core personas drive delivery: **Project Managers** coordinate schedules, risks, and communications; **Product Managers** define what to build and measure success; and **Developers** (alongside QA and other specialists) implement features and ensure quality. Each role has explicit responsibilities and communication patterns, enabling efficient handoffs and reducing ambiguity.

Communication is structured around a predictable cadence designed to maintain alignment without creating overhead. Teams engage in daily standups focused on progress and blockers, weekly PM/PdM syncs, regular delivery standups, monthly stakeholder updates, and ad-hoc escalations as needed. A three-level escalation path (team → PM → Product Lead → Sponsor) ensures that blockers surface quickly and that business-impacting issues reach decision-makers. The organization uses standardized templates for status updates, incident communication, and risk registers, making it easy for distributed teams to stay informed.

Quality and continuous improvement are embedded throughout the lifecycle. Before release, teams ensure passing CI/security scans, unit and integration tests, and smoke tests; acceptance criteria and Definition of Done are defined during planning and validated during execution. After each sprint or milestone, retrospectives convert team insights into measurable change. This emphasis on psychological safety, data-informed decisions, and iterative refinement creates a culture where quality, learning, and delivery reinforce one another.
